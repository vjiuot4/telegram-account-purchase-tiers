4. **Ownership goes to you.** The escrow account transfers full ownership to your Telegram username.
5. **You confirm, and only then is the seller paid.** If the channel doesn't match the description, you open a dispute and the funds are still sitting there.

## Why a transfer can take up to 7 days

This is the detail that trips up buyers planning a launch, and it comes from Telegram itself rather than the marketplace: ownership of a channel or group can only be transferred if the owner's Two-Step Verification password has been active for at least 7 days.

If the seller enabled 2FA recently, the escrow account still gets added as a full admin right away, but Telegram holds the actual transfer until the clock runs out. Accoswap's own description says escrow starts a 7-day countdown in that case and completes the transfer the moment Telegram permits it, with your money staying locked the whole time.

Budget for it. A channel that looks ready today may not be in your name until next week, so don't sell an ad slot on it for tomorrow morning.

## What Accoswap checks, and what you still have to check

Before a listing goes live, sellers must prove they genuinely own and control the channel, and the team reviews member count and activity "to filter out fake or bot-inflated listings." That's a real filter, and it's the reason buying here beats answering a DM from a stranger.

It isn't a guarantee about audience quality, though, and the page doesn't publish a fixed dispute resolution timeline. Do your own reading before you pay:

- Open the last 30 days of posts and compare views per post with the subscriber count. A 40,000-subscriber channel averaging 800 views has a story behind it.
- Look at reactions and comments on recent posts. Real communities argue in the comments.
- Check that the audience's language and geography match whoever you plan to sell to.
- Note the posting cadence. A channel that went quiet three months ago is a restart project, not a running asset.
- Save a copy of the listing description before funding escrow, so any dispute is about documented facts rather than memory.

## Check the listing against these six things

1. Subscriber count, niche and channel type stated on the listing, not promised in chat.
2. Views per post over the last month, measured against subscriber count.
3. Comment and reaction activity on recent posts.
4. Posting continuity, with no long silent gaps.
5. Whether the seller's 2FA has been active for 7+ days, since that sets the transfer clock.
6. A saved copy of the listing text before you fund anything.
